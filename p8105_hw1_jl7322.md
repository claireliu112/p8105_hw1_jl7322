The mean works for the numeric and logical variables. For the logical
variable, R treats `TRUE` as 1 and `FALSE` as 0, so the mean represents
the proportion of values that are `TRUE`. The mean does not work for the
character or factor variables.

Then, convert the logical, charactor, and factor variables to numeric
variables using `as.numeric`.

``` r
as.numeric(pull(problem2_df, sample_positive))
as.numeric(pull(problem2_df, character_vector))
as.numeric(pull(problem2_df, factor_vector))
```

Converting the logical variable to numeric changes `TRUE` to 1 and
`FALSE` to 0. This helps explain why a mean can be calculated for the
logical variable.

Converting the character variable to numeric produces `NA` values, which
means character values cannot be converted to numbers.

Converting the factor variable to numeric produces integer values
corresponding to its factor levels.
